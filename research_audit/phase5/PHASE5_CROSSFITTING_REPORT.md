# Phase 5 Cross-fitting and Model Report

The source train/validation/test splits contain 133,699, 28,667, and 28,909 admissions. A patient appears in exactly one source split (verified: zero patients span splits). Validation selected age spline degrees of freedom from (3, 5, 7); see `PHASE5_VALIDATION_MODEL_SELECTION.csv`. The held-out test split was used once for the stability comparison in `PHASE5_TEST_SPLIT_STABILITY.csv` and the planned holdout implication check in `PHASE5_TEST_SPLIT_INDEPENDENCE_CHECK.csv`; neither was used for tuning. The Phase 4 training diagnostic was not repeated. The held-out check found 1,697/4,371 feature pairs with |r|>=0.10 (median |r|=0.067, maximum=0.873), again inconsistent with the sparse measured-parent-only factorization.

For primary effect estimates, five-fold `GroupKFold` cross-fitting was applied over the eligible analysis population, grouped by `patient_id`. Fold seed: 20261115. Assignment is reproducible and recorded in `PHASE5_CROSSFIT_FOLD_ASSIGNMENTS.csv`. In every fold, nuisance models were fit on other patient groups and predictions were generated only for held-out groups. The full eligible cross-fitted estimates therefore use train, validation, and test patients, as specified by the roadmap; test-only estimates provide the separate final stability check.

Layer 1 estimates the average risk difference (ATE scale) using augmented inverse-probability weighting: logistic exposure propensity, logistic outcome regressions within exposure arms, and the standard doubly robust score. Each exposure uses its own Phase 4 covariate set. Layer 2 estimates feature mean differences with the same AIPW score, logistic MI propensity, and separate linear outcome regressions within MI groups using the Phase 4 adjustment set. Age uses a cubic B-spline selected on validation. Feature outcomes use the existing stored units. Propensities were clipped to [0.01, 0.99]; the clipped fraction and observed range are reported in nuisance diagnostics.

Confidence intervals use patient-level bootstrap resampling of cross-fitted scores (1,000 repetitions, seed 20261115), preserving all admissions from each sampled patient. Nuisance predictions are fixed within the bootstrap, so intervals reflect patient-cluster sampling uncertainty conditional on those fits. Patient-clustered influence-score Wald standard errors provide p-values. BH FDR is applied across the 95 feature-level Wald tests. Physiological feature groups receive cluster-covariance omnibus Wald tests with Holm correction; the technical count feature is reported separately.

All 117 logistic nuisance fits recorded in `PHASE5_CONVERGENCE_AUDIT.csv` converged without warnings or iteration-limit hits (maximum 40 of 500 iterations). Propensity clipping affected 9.96% of CKD exposure rows, 12.37% of prior-MI rows, and 13.63% of Layer 2 rows. Low treated-weight ESS for those analyses signals limited overlap.

These estimators do not resolve unmeasured confounding, selection into the ECG cohort, coded-history misclassification, or unknown MI/ECG timing. Estimates are adjusted observational contrasts; the proposed MI-to-ECG causal direction is not empirically established. The Phase 4 residual dependence remains relevant: correlated feature summaries are not independent evidence, and feature-specific estimates should be interpreted together with the group and FDR results.
